# Frontier after mixed-lens continuation — 9 September 2026

## Completed written result

Every finite nonempty boundary sample of a symmetric parabolic lens has a good
vertex. Witnesses may mix both arcs without any support-pattern restriction.
The point set may be asymmetric, radii may vary by center, and its size is
arbitrary. Maximum |x| gives multiplicity at most two for H<=3/2; minimum |x|
gives at most three for H>=3/2. The normalized lens is x^2<=y<=H-x^2.

This is stronger than the previous opposite-chain averaging theorem WITHIN
THIS GEOMETRY. It does not subsume the previous theorem's allowance of arbitrary
points outside the bounded lens or cycles of different parabolas.

Both simple selectors fail outside their stated regimes: exact five-point
mixed-row controls certify this. The switch value is not claimed optimal for
each selector separately. The deep selector's bound three is attained by an
exact level-set control, not by a configuration rich at every point.

## Not completed

The fixed nine-point seed's two-internally-supported-vertex escape remains
unresolved. A coarse exact graph probe retains 2,676 of 4,500 support/cell cases.
It omits simultaneous circle equations and mutual convexity, so none of those
survivors is a geometric candidate. All 45 zero-old/zero-old cell cases survive.

No reduction sends an arbitrary strictly convex polygon to the symmetric lens.
Mixed rows on unequal-curvature or axis-shifted parabola families, arbitrary
convex polygons, and moving-seed constructions remain outside the new theorem.
No general proof or exact counterexample to Erdős #97 was obtained.

## Evidence status

not by extrapolation from finitely many root counts. Exact finite checks and a
separate implementation are supporting controls. External mathematical review
