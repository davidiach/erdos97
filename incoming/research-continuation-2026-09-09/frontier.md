## Fixed nine-point seed

The co-design packet repairs the old exceptions by adding nine points, but
those new vertices are good. Its nine-addition optimality fixes prescribed
old radii; it is not a lower bound for arbitrary moving-coordinate repairs.

For finite rich caps over the unchanged seed, let I be the new vertices whose
**every** rich radius uses at most one old point. The written classification is:

| Size of I | Retained result |
|---:|---|
| 0 | Only the next recurrence triangle; the six old exceptions remain good |
| 1 | Excluded by the internal-support packet |
| 2 | Excluded by the complete two-free packet |
| 3 | Attained by continuing two recurrence triangles, but not an all-rich repair |

This proves neither that I has bounded size in arbitrary repairs nor that its
members must form a mutually supporting triangle. Moving old coordinates is
outside these exclusions. A cap with at least three internally supported
vertices that also repairs every old exception remains unresolved here.

## Parabolic families

The final-push averaging proof excludes the opposite-chain two-parabola
scaffold and deterministic cycles of parallel parabolas, with one target part
fixed per source part. That theorem does not allow arbitrary mixed targets.

The later mixed-lens theorem removes all witness-pattern restrictions for
finite samples of the specific symmetric lens x^2 <= y <= H - x^2. It does
not treat arbitrary asymmetric parabolas or arbitrary convex polygons.

## Other retained obstructions and controls

The two-copy argument assumes consecutive two-point clusters and at most three
other witness clusters per center. The three-block theorem through 16 vertices
requires all selected witnesses to lie outside the center's own consecutive
block. Neither is a general finite-size exclusion.

The rational rich-neighborhood metric is deliberately globally nonplanar.
Its exact one-center convex realizations and metric/order inequalities expose
a limitation of those necessary conditions, not a near-counterexample in the
Euclidean plane. The specific stored tripled-Danzer patterns have fixed-order
certificates; the entire tripled family is not exhausted.
